What is a Weekly Time Card Calculator?

A weekly time card calculator is a digital timesheet tool that sums daily hours worked across a 7-day week, separates regular hours from overtime hours (based on the FLSA 40-hour threshold), and estimates your gross pay when you enter your hourly rate. It replaces paper time cards and manual timesheet spreadsheets — eliminating the arithmetic errors that frequently appear in payroll submissions.

This calculator is designed for hourly employees, freelancers tracking billable hours, and HR and payroll teams who need to verify weekly timesheet totals before payroll is processed. You can enter hours in decimal format (e.g., 7.5 for 7 hours 30 min) for any day of the week, including partial days and rest days.

Why Should You Use the Weekly Time Card Calculator?

  • Eliminate timesheet arithmetic errors: Manually adding up daily hours across 7 days — especially when some days have partial hours — is surprisingly error-prone. This calculator removes that risk entirely.
  • Instantly identify overtime: See immediately whether your week crossed the 40-hour threshold and exactly how many overtime hours you've accumulated — before you submit the timesheet.
  • Estimate your gross pay: Enter your hourly rate to see an estimated gross pay figure based on your regular hours and overtime hours for the week, with the 1.5× multiplier applied automatically.
  • Payroll verification: HR and payroll teams can use this to quickly verify a submitted timesheet total against employee-reported hours before releasing payroll — a fast sanity check before processing.
  • Freelancer billing support: Freelancers tracking billable hours can use this to sum hours per week and calculate the week's invoice amount, with an instant download to a timestamped CSV.
  • CSV export: Download the completed timesheet as a CSV file — importable into accounting software, project management tools, or sent directly to a client or employer.

How to Use the Weekly Time Card Calculator

  1. Select your currency — formats the gross pay estimate.
  2. Enter your Hourly Rate (optional) — if entered, the calculator will estimate your gross pay including overtime at 1.5×. Leave blank to just sum hours.
  3. Enter hours for each day (Monday through Sunday) — enter in decimal format: e.g., 8 for 8 hours, 7.5 for 7 hours 30 minutes, 0 for a rest day.
  4. Click Calculate Timesheet to see your total weekly hours, regular hours (up to 40), overtime hours (above 40), and estimated gross pay.
  5. Click Download Timesheet to export a CSV version of the week, or Print to produce a physical record.

How Weekly Time Cards and Overtime are Calculated

Total Weekly Hours = Sum of hours entered for Monday through Sunday
Regular Hours = Min(Total Weekly Hours, 40)
Overtime Hours = Max(0, Total Weekly Hours − 40)
Regular Pay = Regular Hours × Hourly Rate
Overtime Pay = Overtime Hours × Hourly Rate × 1.5
Estimated Gross Pay = Regular Pay + Overtime Pay

Worked Example

Hours: Mon 8, Tue 8, Wed 8, Thu 8, Fri 8, Sat 5, Sun 0. Hourly rate: $18/hr.

  • Total Hours = 8+8+8+8+8+5 = 45 hours
  • Regular Hours = 40 | Overtime Hours = 5 hours
  • Regular Pay = 40 × $18 = $720
  • Overtime Pay = 5 × $18 × 1.5 = $135
  • Estimated Gross Pay = $720 + $135 = $855

Timesheet and Time Tracking Best Practices

check_circle

Use Decimal Hours, Not HH:MM

Entering 7.5 instead of 7:30 avoids the most common timesheet input error. Decimal format also makes arithmetic consistent — especially when hours include 15-minute increments (0.25 hr each).

edit_calendar

Log Daily, Not Weekly

Completing your timesheet daily (rather than filling in the whole week on Friday) produces significantly more accurate records. Memory of exact hours fades quickly over a 5-day period.

more_time

Watch for Overtime Creep

Five days of 8 hours is exactly 40 hours — any Saturday or evening hours push you into overtime territory. If you are an employer, monitor weekly totals mid-week to avoid unexpected overtime costs.

download

Save Your CSV

Download and archive your weekly CSV exports as a record of hours worked. These become valuable evidence in any timesheet dispute, and are useful for tax purposes if you are a self-employed contractor.

gavel

FLSA Record-Keeping

US employers must keep records of hours worked by non-exempt employees for at least 2 years (FLSA Section 11). A downloadable weekly time card is a lightweight but compliant starting point for small businesses.

business

Scaling Up?

If you manage multiple employees, manual time cards become unmanageable quickly. An HRMS with built-in timesheet approval workflows, shift scheduling, and automatic payroll integration eliminates the entire manual process.

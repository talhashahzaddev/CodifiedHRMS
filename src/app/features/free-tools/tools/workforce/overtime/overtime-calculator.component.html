What is an Overtime Calculator?

An overtime calculator automatically splits your total weekly hours into regular hours and overtime hours, then applies the correct pay multipliers to compute your total gross wages. For employers, it is an essential payroll verification tool. For employees, it lets you instantly check whether your pay stub reflects the correct time-and-a-half (or double-time) overtime calculation before you query your payroll department.

This calculator follows the US Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) methodology — overtime begins after 40 hours per week at a default multiplier of 1.5×. However, both thresholds are fully customizable, making this tool valid for UK, Indian, Australian, and other global labor frameworks where overtime rules differ.

Why Should You Use the Overtime Calculator?

  • Verify your pay stub accuracy: Employees can cross-check whether their employer has correctly applied the 1.5× or 2× premium to overtime hours — catching payroll errors before they compound.
  • Plan weekly labor costs: Managers and business owners can forecast total gross payroll costs in advance by entering projected hours, avoiding overtime budget overruns.
  • Model different overtime scenarios: Compare the gross pay impact of a 45-hour week vs. a 50-hour week to inform scheduling decisions.
  • FLSA compliance: US employers can use this to verify they are meeting the FLSA minimum overtime rate of 1.5× for all non-exempt employees working over 40 hours per week.
  • Understand your effective hourly rate: The effective hourly rate metric shows what your overtime hours are actually worth in total compensation terms — useful when negotiating hourly contracts.
  • Global applicability: Supports any overtime threshold and multiplier, covering jurisdictions like India (2× after 9 hrs/day under Factories Act) and Australia (various EBA rates).

How to Use the Overtime Calculator

  1. Select your currency — sets the format for all monetary outputs.
  2. Enter Regular Hourly Rate — your base hourly wage before any premium is applied.
  3. Enter Total Hours Worked (Weekly) — your total hours for the week including all overtime.
  4. Set Overtime Starts After — the standard hours limit before overtime kicks in. Defaults to 40 (FLSA). Change to 48 for Indian Factories Act, or 37.5 for some UK contracts.
  5. Set Overtime Rate Multiplier — typically 1.5× ("time and a half") for US FLSA. Use 2.0× for double time, or 1.25× for some UK agreements.
  6. Click Calculate Pay to see the full breakdown: regular pay, overtime pay, total gross pay, overtime as a % of total pay, and effective hourly rate.

How to Calculate Overtime Pay

Regular Hours = Min(Total Hours Worked, Standard Hours Limit)
Overtime Hours = Max(0, Total Hours Worked − Standard Hours Limit)
Regular Pay = Regular Hours × Hourly Rate
Overtime Rate = Hourly Rate × Overtime Multiplier
Overtime Pay = Overtime Hours × Overtime Rate
Total Gross Pay = Regular Pay + Overtime Pay
Effective Hourly Rate = Total Gross Pay ÷ Total Hours Worked

Worked Example

Employee earns $20/hr, worked 45 hours in a week. Standard limit: 40 hours. Multiplier: 1.5×.

  • Regular Hours = 40 | Overtime Hours = 45 − 40 = 5 hours
  • Regular Pay = 40 × $20 = $800
  • Overtime Rate = $20 × 1.5 = $30/hr
  • Overtime Pay = 5 × $30 = $150
  • Total Gross Pay = $800 + $150 = $950
  • Effective Hourly Rate = $950 ÷ 45 = $21.11/hr

Understanding Your Overtime Data

check_circle

0–5 Overtime Hours/Week

Moderate overtime. Common and legally compliant as long as the correct 1.5× rate is applied. Monitor trends to ensure it doesn't creep toward unsustainable levels.

warning

5–15 Overtime Hours/Week

Significant overtime. At 15 extra hours/week, an employee is effectively working a 37.5% longer week than contracted. Consider whether additional headcount is more cost-effective than sustained premium pay.

cancel

15+ Overtime Hours/Week

Chronic overtime. Beyond the direct pay cost, research consistently shows that sustained overtime above 50 hours/week reduces hourly output — often making overtime less productive per pound or dollar spent.

pie_chart

Overtime % of Total Pay

If overtime routinely represents more than 20% of your gross pay, your base hourly rate may be too low relative to your actual working pattern — this is worth raising in a salary review conversation.

speed

Effective Hourly Rate

Compare your effective hourly rate to your regular rate — the premium is the true financial value of staying late. Use this figure when deciding whether to take on additional overtime shifts.

business

Employer Perspective

If overtime is budgeted at $0 but regularly consumed, it is likely hidden in your payroll variance. Calculate the annualized overtime cost and compare it to the cost of a part-time hire or contract worker.

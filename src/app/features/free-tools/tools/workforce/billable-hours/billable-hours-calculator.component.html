What is a Billable Hours Calculator?

A billable hours calculator measures what percentage of total hours worked are spent on chargeable, revenue-generating activities. It calculates the Billable Percentage — one of the most critical KPIs for consulting, legal, IT services, agencies, and any firm that bills clients by time.

Beyond the headline percentage, this calculator computes: the exact billing revenue generated, revenue per employee, billable hours per employee, the target billable hours needed to hit your goal, and — critically — the revenue potential lost from hours that fall short of target. This gives both operational and financial visibility in a single calculation.

Why Should You Use the Billable Hours Calculator?

  • Measure revenue-generating efficiency: Billable % directly measures what fraction of your team's effort translates into client revenue — the most fundamental performance measure for time-based billing businesses.
  • Quantify the cost of non-billable time: The revenue potential lost output converts non-billable hours into a financial figure, making the case for reducing admin overhead or improving project coverage.
  • Set and track targets: Enter your target billable % and the calculator shows the exact gap in hours and the revenue at stake — giving your operations team a clear, measurable objective.
  • Benchmark per employee: Revenue per employee and billable hours per employee enable fair comparison across team members or service lines with different billing rates.
  • Identify patterns in non-billable time: Running this weekly or monthly reveals whether non-billable time is growing — often the first signal of process inefficiency, scope creep, or rework.
  • Support pricing decisions: Consistent billable % data informs whether your billing rate reflects the true cost of the non-billable overhead embedded in every client engagement.

How to Use the Billable Hours Calculator

  1. Select your currency — used to display revenue and revenue-lost figures.
  2. Enter Total Hours Worked — all hours worked in the period, including billable and non-billable time.
  3. Enter Non-Billable Hours — admin, internal meetings, training, business development, rework, and other non-client-facing time.
  4. Enter Billing Rate per Hour — your standard hourly client billing rate (or the weighted average if rates vary by project).
  5. Enter Number of Employees — used to calculate per-employee averages. Enter 1 for an individual analysis.
  6. Enter Target Billable % — your firm's target (e.g., 75%). The calculator checks whether you met it and shows the gap.
  7. Click Calculate. All results appear immediately, including the target check and revenue gap.

How to Calculate Billable Hours Percentage

Billable Hours = Total Hours Worked − Non-Billable Hours
Billable Percentage (%) = (Billable Hours ÷ Total Hours Worked) × 100
Billing Revenue = Billable Hours × Billing Rate per Hour
Revenue per Employee = Billing Revenue ÷ Number of Employees
Target Billable Hours = Total Hours × (Target % ÷ 100)
Gap Hours = max(0, Target Billable Hours − Actual Billable Hours)
Revenue Potential Lost = Gap Hours × Billing Rate per Hour

Worked Example

Team of 5 consultants. Total hours worked: 160 hrs. Non-billable: 35 hrs. Rate: $150/hr. Target: 75%.

  • Billable Hours = 160 − 35 = 125 hrs
  • Billable % = (125 ÷ 160) × 100 = 78.1% 🟢 Above target
  • Revenue = 125 × $150 = $18,750
  • Revenue per Employee = $18,750 ÷ 5 = $3,750
  • Target Billable Hours = 160 × 75% = 120 hrs ✅ Actual (125) exceeds target

What Your Billable Hours Data Could Be Telling You

check_circle

≥ 80% Billable — High Billability

Excellent revenue efficiency. Ensure non-billable time is not being suppressed artificially. Invest in non-billable activities (training, BD) to sustain long-term capability.

info

70–79% — Moderate Billability

Within acceptable range for most professional services firms. Small improvements in scheduling and admin reduction can push this to the next tier.

warning

Below 70% — Revenue Leakage

Significant non-billable overhead. Audit what the 30%+ non-billable hours consist of. Common culprits: excessive meetings, admin rework, scope creep that can't be billed, and time lost to context switching.

person

Revenue per Employee Below Market

If revenue per employee is below your industry benchmark, either the billing rate is below market or billable % is too low. Use both levers — improving billability is often faster than renegotiating rates.

money_off

Large Revenue Potential Lost

Use the revenue potential lost figure to build the ROI case for: hiring dedicated admin support, automating timesheet submission, reducing internal meeting frequency, or improving project pipeline coverage.

trending_up

Compare Billable % Month-on-Month

A single month's result has limited value. Track monthly to spot seasonal patterns and measure the impact of process changes. Most improving firms show a consistent 1–2% monthly gain over 6 months before plateau.
